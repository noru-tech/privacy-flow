# PF004: Special-category data (Art. 9 or 10) reaches an external sink

**A flow of special-category data (health, biometric, genetic, racial or ethnic origin, political opinion, religious belief, sexual orientation, criminal history) to any sink outside the system is a finding.**

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Rule ID | `PF004` |
| Default severity | `high` (error (SARIF)) |
| Fails the default policy (`fail_on: medium`) | yes |
| Fixtures | [`tests/fixtures/py/pf004-fail`](../../tests/fixtures/py/pf004-fail) (fails), [`tests/fixtures/py/pf004-pass`](../../tests/fixtures/py/pf004-pass) (passes), and the Python equivalents |

## Why it matters

Article 9 data may be processed only under one of a short list of conditions, and Article 10
data only under official authority or Union or member-state law. Sending it to a third party is
the step most likely to need a condition nobody checked. The rule reads the category from the
vendored list of Fideslang keys the two articles cover
(`vendor/taxonomy/special_categories.json`), matched by prefix: `user.health_and_medical` covers
`user.health_and_medical.record_id`.

## When it fires

- the flow's category is special (by prefix against the vendored list), and
- the sink's class is external (anything but `log` and `browser_storage`), and the call is not a
  relative URL to the application's own origin.

The list reads Article 9 broadly: all of `user.biometric` is listed, although Article 9 covers
biometric data only when processed to identify someone, because the analysis cannot see the
purpose. Narrow it with a disposition and a rationale, never by editing the list. A declared
processor does not suppress PF004: declaring the vendor is not a legal condition for the data.

## Controls

- GDPR Art. 9 — special categories of personal data.
- GDPR Art. 10 — personal data relating to criminal convictions and offences.

These mappings are identifiers with Noru's own short gloss; no normative text from any standard is
quoted. They are a guide for control owners, not a compliance claim, and are pending review by a
privacy lawyer or Noru's compliance lead before the first release
([control mapping](../control-mapping.md)).

## How to fix

- Do not send special-category data to third parties without an Article 9 condition (or Article 10
  authority) recorded for that exact processing.
- If it is required and lawful, record the condition in the disposition's rationale.

## Recording a disposition

A finding is never deleted. To record a human decision about it, edit only the finding's
`disposition` in `.privacy-flow/flows.json` (`accepted`, `remediated` or `false_positive`, with
`owner`, `decided_at`, `rationale`, and `remediated_at` or an optional `expires_at`), then run
`piiflow check .privacy-flow/flows.json --as-of YYYY-MM-DD`. The date is required because the
machine clock is never read. A disposition suppresses the finding from the threshold decision on
that date and leaves the finding, its flow and the summary intact; the next `scan` carries it over
by finding ID, which does not depend on line numbers. Exact rules:
[dispositions](../output.md#dispositions).

```json
{
  "status": "accepted",
  "owner": "privacy@example.com",
  "decided_at": "2026-10-01",
  "expires_at": "2026-12-31",
  "rationale": "Art. 9(2)(h): processing by health professionals under the care contract; vendor is the clinic's EHR.",
  "remediated_at": null
}
```

To change how the rule is enforced rather than record a one-off decision, set its severity or
disable it under `policy.rules.PF004` in [`.privacy-flow.yml`](../configuration.md#policy).
